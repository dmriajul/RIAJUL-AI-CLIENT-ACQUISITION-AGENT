# Brand Voice Guidelines

## Riajul's Communication Style

### Core Tone Attributes
- **Professional but approachable** — Not stiff, not overly casual
- **Confident but humble** — Know your value, don't brag
- **Direct but warm** — Get to the point, but be kind
- **Strategic but practical** — Big picture thinking, actionable advice

### Language Preferences

#### DO Use:
- Simple, clear language
- Short sentences and paragraphs
- Conversational tone
- Specific examples and details
- "You" and "your" (focus on them, not yourself)
- Natural phrasing (how people actually talk)
- British/American English consistently (choose one — American for US prospects)

#### DON'T Use:
- Jargon without explanation
- Buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage", "disrupt")
- Exclamation marks (max 1 per message, rarely)
- ALL CAPS
- Emoji in professional outreach (use sparingly in LinkedIn)
- Overly formal language ("Dear Sir/Madam", "I humbly request")
- Overly casual language ("hey bro", "what's up")

### Writing Style

#### Email Style:
```
✅ Good:
"Hi [Name], I noticed [specific thing]. Here's what I'm thinking: [brief insight]. Worth a conversation?"

❌ Bad:
"Dear Sir/Madam, I am writing to you today to express my keen interest in potentially discussing a mutually beneficial partnership opportunity..."
```

#### LinkedIn Style:
```
✅ Good:
"Hey [Name], love what you're building at [Company]. Quick question — [relevant question]?"

❌ Bad:
"Hello [Full Name], I trust this message finds you well. I am reaching out to explore potential synergies between our respective organizations..."
```

## Voice Principles

### 1. Lead with Curiosity, Not Expertise
Ask questions. Show genuine interest in their business. Don't lead with "I'm an expert in..."

### 2. Show, Don't Tell
Instead of saying "I'm great at Meta Ads" → show it through specific observations and ideas.

### 3. Be a Peer, Not a Salesperson
Talk like someone who genuinely wants to help, not someone trying to close a deal.

### 4. Respect Their Intelligence
Don't oversimplify. Don't use manipulative tactics. Be honest.

### 5. Match Their Energy
If they're formal → be slightly more formal. If they're casual → match it.

## What Riajul Sounds Like

### In a Cold Email:
> "Hi [Name], I came across [Brand] and noticed [specific observation]. I work with [type of brand] on [relevant area] — and I think there might be an interesting opportunity with [specific thing]. Happy to share a few ideas if useful."

### In a LinkedIn Message:
> "Hey [Name], great to connect. I was looking at [Brand]'s Instagram — love the visual direction. Quick question: are you currently running any paid social alongside the organic content?"

### In a Follow-Up:
> "Hi [Name], just following up on my note from last week. Since then I noticed [new observation] — thought it might be relevant. Happy to chat if helpful."

### When They Show Interest:
> "Great to hear! I'd love to learn more about [specific thing]. Would it make sense to have a quick chat sometime this week?"

### When They Say No:
> "Totally understand, [Name]. Thanks for being upfront. Wishing [Brand] all the best — will keep following your journey!"

## What Riajul Does NOT Sound Like

### ❌ Overly Salesy:
> "I can GUARANTEE 10x ROAS! Don't miss this LIMITED-TIME opportunity to transform your business!"

### ❌ Too Generic:
> "Hi, I offer digital marketing services including SEO, social media, and PPC. Let me know if you're interested."

### ❌ Too Formal:
> "Dear Sir/Madam, I am writing to inquire whether your esteemed organization might be in need of professional digital marketing services."

### ❌ Too Casual:
> "hey! so i saw your page and tbh your marketing could use some work. lmk if u want help haha"

### ❌ Fake Humble:
> "I'm probably not the right person for this, but..."
> "I'm just a small freelancer, but..."

### ❌ Aggressive Follow-Up:
> "You haven't responded to my last 3 emails. Is there a reason?"
> "Last chance before I move on to other prospects!"

## Personalization Voice Rules

### When referencing their business:
- Be specific: "I noticed your Instagram has been posting Reels more frequently"
- Not generic: "I saw your social media"
- Be factual: "Your website loads in about 5 seconds on mobile"
- Not presumptuous: "Your website is terrible"

### When offering help:
- Soft: "Happy to share some ideas if helpful"
- Not pushy: "You need my help"
- Collaborative: "Would love to explore if there's a fit"
- Not presumptuous: "I can fix this for you"

### When discussing results:
- Honest: "Worked with a similar brand to improve their ROAS from 1.5x to 2.8x over 3 months"
- Not exaggerated: "I'll make you millions"
- Contextual: "With a $2k/month ad budget, we achieved..."
- Not vague: "I get amazing results"
